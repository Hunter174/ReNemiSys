# Development-only retreat requests (ReNemiSys #13, IN PROGRESS)

**Not a group-contagion mechanic.** A retreating actor requests assistance from the nearest ally obstructing its outward path. That ally may consent to a short, game-authorized sidestep or refuse; when it refuses, the requester may take a separately collision-checked sideways counter-route or guard. A refusal does not magically revoke an independently legal route. At most two local hops propagate a *space request*, not another actor's fear or policy weights. No SaLMon/LLM call, network wait, persistent trait change, new epiphany or per-character model is involved. The existing v3 combat policy still proposes ordinary tactics; authored, inspectable recipient utility provides the snap response. Parameters in `retreat_coordination.gd` are untuned authored values; #14 owns any future offline search.

The request contains stable actor ID, recipient ID, current game revision, next-decision-tick expiry, and outward vector. Recipient decisions use its visible HP, current action/commitment, courage and distance to player as *inputs*, never modify them. A low-HP or tactically committed ally can refuse. A proposal to yield contains collision-checked targets for up to two allies; Godot checks the live actors, legal response, wall bounds and unique reservations **again**, then applies the entire plan or none. The temporary move ends after 0.36s or death/unload/revision invalidation. Godot physics resolves real `CharacterBody2D` collisions; there is no teleport through friends, enemies or walls. A stale/expired answer is rejected, leaving the requester with a legal guard. Swept corridors are checked against the player as well as allies, including when the target itself is clear; the game rechecks proposed recipient paths and disallows reassigning a still-yielding ally to a *different* requester. One requester can renew its own short-lived consent. A wall-blocked outward lane does not revoke an independent sideways exit. Request/response runs synchronously on the next 0.2s action update, not in an LLM queue; fast response is not guaranteed under arbitrarily blocking game code.

Change `ARENA_RETREAT_REQUESTS=0` for the paired dogpile. Cases: `approve`, `deny`, `counter`, `relay`, `edge` (both sideways lanes blocked), `edge_open` (one independent lane open), `multiple` (separate helpers), `contested` (shared helper), `death`, `unload`, and `natural`; only development seeds **900–907** are allowed. Scripted fixtures freeze the player and force a legal retreat/guard intent to isolate actual movement—NOT learned combat success. `natural` uses unchanged v3 policy proposals and moving player, and is only a development comparison. Neither mode can collect weights, use diagnostics, open the frozen 610xx blind suite, run on other teams or modify the main game. The frozen #4 movement contract remains opt-out; changed movement needs a *different* blind protocol before any generalization claim.

Run:

- `Godot-4.4.1 --headless --path demos/adaptive_arena --script res://retreat_request_check.gd` (nine pure contract cases: consent, two-hop relay, refusal, counter, blocked/open wall, swept player corridor, alternate player-safe route and stale/expired revision).
- `python demos/adaptive_arena/retreat_physics_check.py --godot /path/to/Godot_v4.4.1-stable_win64_console.exe` (17 real physics cases, including 8/10/20 actors, scripted collision obstruction, blocked/open wall, separate and contested simultaneous requesters, death/unload and forced stale/expiry). The off columns are earlier opt-out measurements (unchanged policy/movement) and **not fresh matched runs** for this revision. 92 full-active action passes per enabled run. Two more enabled 20-agent samples measured p95/p99 **1.178/2.412 and 0.844/2.653 ms**; **two of three p99 samples exceed 2.5ms**, and one p95 exceeds 1.2ms. An earlier 10-agent enabled run reached p99 3.455ms, and another frame reached 32.617ms. These are small development samples, **not a hard CPU deadline**; the provisional #7-style 20-agent tail budget is currently unmet by this changed coordination path. A later **single paired** rendered 20-agent opt-out/opt-in diagnostic (after adding development-only timing counters) measured full-action p95/p99 **0.626/0.866 -> 1.005/1.621ms**. Opt-in policy/observation alone was **0.607/1.289ms**, and the local request handler was **0.349/0.450ms**; percentiles cannot simply be added. Its frame p99 was **18.213 -> 18.160ms**. The added handler costs time, but a single pair cannot attribute every earlier 4.190ms action tail to it or exclude host/GC variation. More profiling/overload work is required before a production gate.

Eight previously opened **development-only** natural 10-agent seeds (900–907) on the **earlier prototype**, fresh processes for each mode and an identical authored moving-opponent schedule: consent off **798 player damage / 4 goblin defeats**; consent on **848 player damage / 6 goblin defeats**. Requests with consent on: 3 approvals, 170 refusals, 10 counters, 193 unobstructed exits. Thus the scripted egress success did **not** establish better natural group survival; some individual seeds lost damage or more goblins. More critically, ordinary Godot `CharacterBody2D` fights have residual >1px body penetration **even with coordination disabled**: 219 body-pair/frame samples off (maximum 1.77px) and 72 on (maximum 1.80px). Scripted cases had zero. With the *current* consent code, the **same already-opened** 900–907 starts were rerun in separate, deterministic accelerated physics processes: opt-out **701 damage / 6 goblins defeated / 178 >1px pair-frame overlap samples (max 1.79px)**; opt-in **748 / 5 / 31 (max 1.86px)**. Opt-in yielded twice, refused 148 times and countered nine times. These are development regressions, **not a new held-out quality test** or proof of a survival benefit; some seeds still lost goblins or damage, and both modes penetrate. A bounded dev-only trial lowering Godot `CharacterBody2D.safe_margin` to 0.001 **also retained >1px overlap in both modes**; the trial was removed, not adopted as a physics fix. A separate **temporary 120Hz physics-tick** trial on four already-opened natural seeds reduced the opt-out >1px contact count to zero but still had **four opt-in >1px samples** on seed 900 (max 1.11px); decision counts, hit trajectories and defeats changed, so it cannot be compared to the 60Hz combat contract as if only contact resolution changed. One rendered 20-agent 120Hz scripted sample measured action p95/p99 **0.962/1.679ms**, frame p99 **19.282ms** and maximum frame **26.446ms**; one run proves no worst-case deadline. This trial was also removed. Development-only logs now retain eight example contact pairs and separate observation/policy versus request-handler timing, with zero impact on the frozen opt-out blind movement. The solver/crowd gap and observed rendered 20-agent action tails remain open in #13; neither is a proven collision-free/main-game result. Do not tune #14 to these opened seeds or evaluate coordination on 610xx.
